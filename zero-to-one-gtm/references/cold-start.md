# Cold start

The founder is a researcher. There is a hypothesis about a problem, not a solution to pitch. The useful outcome of an early conversation is disconfirming the hypothesis.

Work outward one ring at a time. Do not start with strangers.

## Rings

**Ring 0 — Own experience.** Write the workflow, triggers, consequences, and workarounds. This calibrates every later question.

**Ring 1 — People already known.** Former colleagues, friends in the industry, people from past companies who held the target role. Not a prospect list.

Ask:

> I'm researching how [role] handles [specific workflow]. You've done this — can I ask you some questions about how it worked at [company]? 15–20 minutes, no pitch, just trying to understand the problem.

**Ring 2 — Second-degree introductions.** At the end of every Ring 1 conversation:

> Who else do you know who deals with this regularly? I'd love to hear how they handle it differently.

**Ring 3 — Communities where the target already gathers.** Participate. Do not pitch. Answer questions, ask questions, comment with substance. Discovery conversations come after presence.

**Ring 4 — Strangers, warm by context.** Only after Rings 1–3 have produced sharp language and a repeated pattern.

> I've been talking to [role] at companies like [comparable companies] about [specific problem]. I noticed [pattern]. I'm trying to understand if this is common — would you be open to a 15-minute conversation?

## Interview questions

Use past behavior. Do not pitch.

- Walk me through the last time this happened.
- What triggered it?
- How did you handle it?
- What did that cost in time, money, risk, or lost opportunity?
- What have you already tried?
- Who cared, who approved, and who did the work?
- Where did you look for an answer?
- What happened when you did nothing?

## Research frame, not sales frame

| Do not send | Send |
|---|---|
| Can I show you what we're building? | Can I ask how you handle [workflow] today? |
| Would you use a tool that does X? | Walk me through the last time [problem] happened. |
| We have a solution for Y. | I keep hearing that Y is painful — is that true for you? |
| Can I get 30 minutes? | 15 minutes, no pitch — I'm just trying to understand the problem. |

## First week

1. List 10 people already known who are close to the problem. Not prospects.
2. Send 5 research-frame messages, in whatever channel is natural for that relationship.
3. Join 2–3 communities where the target role gathers. Lurk for a week. Note their language.
4. After every conversation, ask for one introduction.

## Share post

Three parts, in this order:

1. The problem, in their language. One or two sentences. Do not describe the solution yet.
2. What this is and why it exists. Two or three sentences. "I kept running into X, so I built Y."
3. One invitation: a reaction, whether they have the problem, or how they handle it today.

Do not write a launch announcement. Do not list features. Do not ask them to sign up, subscribe, or buy.

## After they engage

Prefer, in order: a scheduled 15-minute conversation, an async exchange about their workflow, a follow or connection, or nothing. If they say "cool" and leave, let them.

Someone says this is useful:

> Thanks — curious what brought you to it. Were you dealing with [the problem] recently?

Someone describes their version of the problem:

> That's really similar to what I kept running into. Would you be up for a quick call? I'm trying to understand how different people handle this. 15 minutes, no agenda beyond learning how your workflow looks.

Someone asks for a feature:

> Interesting — that tells me your workflow looks a bit different from what I assumed. Can I ask you a couple questions about how you're using it?

Someone shares it:

> Appreciate the share. If [tagged person] ends up using it, I'd love to hear their take.

## Follow up within 24 hours

After a call:

> Thanks for the time today. The way you described [specific thing] was really helpful — I hadn't thought about [specific insight]. If it's okay, I'd love to follow up in a couple weeks as I work on [related thing]. Also — you mentioned [colleague] deals with something similar. Would you be comfortable making an intro?

After a useful DM:

> Really appreciate you walking me through that. Would you be open to a quick call sometime? I'd love to dig into [specific detail] a bit more. Totally informal — just trying to understand the problem better.

## Builder-first

When a product instinct already exists and there are no customers, build the smallest useful thing and use it to start conversations. Do not finish the product and then go looking for strangers.

- Variant 1: ship a small tool, template, calculator, script, or checklist in days. The people who use it have the problem. Ask what they were dealing with.
- Variant 2: find two or three people through Rings 1–2 and build with them. They give workflow time. They are not being sold a finished product.
- Variant 3: write about the problem where the audience already is. The why, more than the feature list.
- Variant 4: if the founder has the pain, get the thing useful in their own workflow, then share it as "I had this, I built this, anyone else?"

If building has continued for more than two weeks with no conversation with someone who has the pain, say that the search has drifted back into isolation.
