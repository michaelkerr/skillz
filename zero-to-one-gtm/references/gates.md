# Gates

A gate answers what evidence changed, what decision follows, and what resource is now justified. Completing a list of documents is not a pass.

Update `stage` only after the user confirms that the current gate's exit evidence is actually true. `gate_check` reports gaps. It does not edit `stage`.

## Frame — gate `coherent-test`

Exit evidence: a specific customer, a painful situation, the current alternative, a testable promise, and a proposed commitment.

Stay here when the target is everyone, or when value is a feature description.

## Discover — gate `problem-evidence`

Exit evidence: a narrow segment repeatedly shows the problem, can recount a recent example, already spends resources or accepts the consequence, and can be reached through an identifiable place or relationship.

If the evidence splits across unrelated problems, narrow or change the segment. Do not open Design.

## Design — gate `test-readiness`

Exit evidence: the team can name prospects, make one unambiguous promise, ask for one concrete next step, deliver the proposed first value, and capture every result.

Do not build a brand system, a marketing stack, a content engine, or a large campaign to pass this gate.

## Validate — gate `demand-evidence`

Exit evidence: more than one ICP-fit prospect independently takes a costly next step, not merely a call. The team knows which trigger and message created interest, why people advanced or stalled, and which disqualifiers predict wasted effort.

## Commit — gate `commercial-evidence`

A customer meets the commitment standard for the model:

- B2B or enterprise: paid design partnership, paid pilot, signed order, or contract.
- Consumer subscription: payment and meaningful use beyond signup.
- Marketplace: a completed transaction, with each side tracked separately.
- Transactional product: purchase followed by fulfillment or consumption.
- Freemium or product-led: repeated core use plus a credible monetization event. A signup is not a customer.
- Regulated or long-cycle: paid feasibility work, a procurement milestone, or an LOI with concrete obligations, labeled as short of revenue.

Exit evidence: the buyer exchanges money or another defined costly commitment, the team knows what was purchased and by whom, and there is an agreed path from evaluation to ongoing use.

## Activate — gate `value-evidence`

Exit evidence: the customer reaches the predefined first-value event, acknowledges the result, and shows continued behavior that fits the model. That means ongoing use, a second transaction, renewal intent backed by action, expansion, or a qualified referral.

Separate product-delivered value from founder-delivered service in the evidence ledger.

## Learn — gate `repeatability-hypothesis`

Exit evidence: a dominant pattern is visible across a meaningful subset of the customers. The team can say who to target, how to reach them, why they buy, how they commit, and how they reach value.

This permits a repeatability test with the next cohort. It is not permission to scale.

## Decisions

At a weekly review, recommend exactly one:

- Persevere: the hypothesis gained stronger evidence. Rerun with a comparable cohort.
- Modify: keep the segment and problem. Change one element, such as message, offer, price, or onboarding.
- Narrow: focus on the subsegment or use case with the strongest pull.
- Pivot: replace a foundational hypothesis. Customer, problem, value, channel, or model.
- Stop: evidence repeatedly contradicts a critical assumption, and no adjacent hypothesis justifies more investment.

Write `decision` in `STATUS.md` only after the user confirms the recommendation.
